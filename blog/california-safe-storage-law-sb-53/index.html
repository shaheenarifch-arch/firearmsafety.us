California safe storage law (SB 53): what changed in 2026

How California's SB 53 secure storage rule works at home, what counts as secure storage and practical ways to comply.

California's Senate Bill 53 took effect on January 1, 2026. It extends the state's older child-access rules to every home. In short, if a firearm in a residence is not being carried or kept under the control of a lawful user, it has to be stored securely.

What counts as secure storage

The law defines it as being kept in, locked by, or disabled with a certified firearm safety device or a secure gun safe. In practice, that means one of these:

  • A DOJ-approved gun safe or lockbox.
  • A certified cable lock or trigger lock fitted so the firearm cannot fire.

Hiding a firearm in a drawer, a closet or under a mattress is not secure storage.

Penalties

First and second violations are infractions with fines of up to $500. Later violations can be charged as misdemeanors. Separate, more serious laws apply if a child or prohibited person gets hold of a firearm.

A simple storage plan

  1. Choose the container for how you use the firearm: a quick-access lockbox for a home-defense handgun, a larger safe for long guns.
  2. Unload before storing unless you have a considered reason not to, and lock ammunition separately when you can.
  3. Control access. Do not share codes with children, and do not hide keys where they can be found.
  4. Practice opening it in the dark and under pressure so secure storage does not feel like a trade-off.
  5. Re-check when guests stay or when life changes, such as a new baby or a teenager in the house.

This is general information, not legal advice. Read the current rules on the California Department of Justice website and ask a qualified attorney about your own situation.

Common questions

When did SB 53 take effect?

January 1, 2026.

What is the penalty for not storing a firearm securely in California?

The first and second offenses are infractions with fines of up to $500. Later offenses can be misdemeanors.

Does a cable lock count as secure storage?

A certified firearm safety device, such as an approved cable lock fitted so the firearm cannot fire, meets the definition.
